Levels & guide

Levels - in teaching order

What this is

A Bengali word puzzle, one akshara at a time. Drag across the letters to spell a word; each word you find writes itself into the grid above. Levels, and each one is a single akshara: the board for কা is কাক, কাঠ, কান, কাচ, কাপ, কাঠি - six words, every one of them starting with কা.

That is the point of it. Bengali is taught as a letter and then the same letter under each vowel sign - ক, কা, কি, কু, কে, কো, কাঁ - and a level per akshara is a level per step of that series. A child who can read কাক can read কাঠ and কান without being taught them twice, and the level is where that clicks.

Levels run in alphabet order by base letter, and inside a letter the bare form comes first, then its vowel signs, then a level of mixed signs for the letters whose single aksharas have too few words to fill a wheel on their own. ঙ, ঞ and ণ are skipped - no Bengali word begins with them - and neither do ড়, ঢ় or য়.

Every letter that can begin a Bengali word now has a level, the full স্বরবর্ণ run included: অ আ ই ঈ উ ঊ ঋ এ ঐ ও ঔ. Only ঙ, ঞ, ণ, ড়, ঢ় and য় have none, and no Bengali word begins with any of them.

ঐ and ঔ are the weakest levels in the game and it is worth knowing why. Word-initial ঐ and ঔ in Bengali are almost entirely abstract nouns carrying heavy conjuncts - ঐক্য, ঐতিহ্য, ঔদ্ধত্য, ঔজ্জ্বল্য - so those two levels teach the letter and nothing a child can picture. There is no concrete alternative in the language to put there instead.

A board is not always one joined-up crossword. Words cross wherever they share a letter, but on a level where every word starts with the same akshara the only cell any two can share is that one - and a cell takes two words, not six. So a board is one, two or three islands, and the words in each still cross.

Some aksharas come round twice - কা, then কা again later - which is more practice on the same step rather than a new one. Every word is a concrete thing a picture could show, or a number, a colour or an everyday action; the handful that are not carry a note saying how to draw them.

Everything is Bangladeshi Bengali: পানি rather than জল, চাচা and ফুফু rather than কাকা and পিসি. No word is ever set as a puzzle twice. Nothing to install, no sign-up, no ads - your progress is saved in this browser.

How to play

  • dragPress a letter and drag across the others. Sliding back onto the previous letter un-picks it.
  • clickPrefer clicking? Tap letters one at a time, then hit ✓ on the word - or press Enter. Esc clears.
  • finishClearing a level moves straight to the next one - no button, no waiting. The confetti plays across the change.
  • ‹ ›The arrows step to the previous or next level, and wrap around at either end. The level grid jumps straight to any level, and is coloured by stage of the syllabus. Any level you open starts blank, so an old one can always be played again.
  • ♪Every word is spoken aloud as you find it, and there is no button for it - hearing the word is the point, not a setting. Bengali spelling hides the sound: ব and ভ, শ and ষ and স are each one sound but different letters. On a device with no Bengali voice the game stays silent rather than reading Bengali in an English voice.
  • ◈A hint reveals one letter from a word you haven't solved. Free, and no limit - there is nothing to count and nothing to spend.

One tile = one akshara

মাছমা + ছvowel sign
বন্ধুব + ন্ধুconjunct
বাড়িবা + ড়িnukta + sign

Bengali isn't one letter per character - মাছ is three code points but two letter units. The game splits on those units, so a tile is always something a reader recognises.

Every board is generated from its word list when you open the level, not hand-drawn: words are placed so that they cross wherever they can, and no accidental word ever appears on the grid. Every word is a concrete thing a picture could show - a thing, a number, a colour or an everyday action - and carries an English gloss, so the whole set can be illustrated. No word is set as a puzzle twice - not once across the whole game. Every level is checked to be about its own akshara: a কা level is all কা words, and the check fails if one is not. Tiles hold aksharas - the letter units a Bengali reader sees - so মাছ is two tiles, মা and ছ, and the conjunct ন্ধু in বন্ধু is one. A wheel carries a letter twice when a word needs it twice, as ঘুঘু and বাবা do, and the two are kept apart on the ring so a drag from one to the other does not run over its twin. Bengali text is rendered with the embedded font, and every word is spoken as it lands, using the device's own Bengali voice where there is one. This is the web build and it is the whole product; an Android app in the repository is parked and does not share this layout code.
